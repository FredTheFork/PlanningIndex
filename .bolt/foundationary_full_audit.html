Trust & Social Proof
The FAQ page answers are completely emptycritical
Every single FAQ question on your /faq page is there but has no answer — the accordion items have headings but blank bodies. This is the most damaging issue on the site right now. Someone who is hesitating before buying goes to the FAQ to be reassured. They find nothing. They leave.
  • Add full, honest written answers to every single question
  • Especially: "Is this a legal service?", "What if I'm not happy?", "Is there a guarantee?" — these are the make-or-break ones
  • Each answer should be 2–5 sentences, plain English, no hedging
I could read the HTML of your FAQ page and every accordion item was empty. This needs to be the very first thing you fix.
Testimonials have no surnames, no photos, no verifiabilitycritical
You have three testimonials — Sarah M., James T., Priya K. — shown with initials avatars. This is the pattern of fabricated reviews. Even if they're entirely genuine, they look made up to any sceptical visitor.
  • Get real clients to provide a photo, their full name, and their business name/URL
  • Link each testimonial to the client's LinkedIn profile or website — instant verifiability
  • Video testimonials are 10x more powerful — even a 30-second Loom from one real client beats three text reviews
  • Add a Trustpilot widget or Google Reviews embed — third-party verified reviews carry far more weight
One verified review with a real face and a real business name is worth more than ten anonymous initials.

The homepage shows "4.2M UK Sole Traders" as a stat — that's a market size number, not a trust signal. What's missing is how many have used Foundationary.
  • Add a live or updated "X sole traders protected" counter — even if it starts at 47, it's real and it grows
  • "Join 200+ UK sole traders who..." reads entirely differently to a market size statistic
  • Show it in the hero and near the CTA buttons
No founder face or identity on the About pagehigh impact
The About page is beautifully written but entirely faceless. There is no photo, no name, no LinkedIn link — nothing that puts a human behind the business. For a service built entirely on trust, this is a significant weakness.
  • Add a founder section: real photo, your name, one paragraph about why you built this
  • Link to your personal LinkedIn
  • People buy from people. The brand voice is excellent — now put a face to it.
The writing on the About page is some of the best on the site. It just needs a human attached to it.
No press mentions, badges or third-party recognitionmedium

  • Apply for FSB, Enterprise Nation, or StartUp Britain recognition — many are free and give you a badge
  • A Trustpilot "Excellent" banner is one of the highest-converting trust elements in UK e-commerce
Conversion & Purchase Flow
The "Get Started" and "Get My Pack" buttons on the homepage go to #anchor, not /checkoutcritical
I can see from the page source that several CTA buttons use href="#" — they scroll to nowhere. The primary action on your homepage is broken for users who click the hero CTA.
  • Every CTA button that says "Get Started", "Get My Pack", "Get My Business Foundations Pack" must link directly to /checkout
  • Check every single page — the pricing page, what's included page, and homepage all have this issue in multiple places
This is a direct revenue leak. Fix this before anything else conversion-related.
No money-back guarantee or risk reversalcritical
Your refund policy is currently: no refunds after submission. This is completely standard for a done-for-you service — but it creates friction at the point of purchase. Someone spending £79 with a brand they've never heard of needs risk reversal.
  • Add a "7-day satisfaction policy" — if the documents don't reflect your business accurately, you'll fix them. This costs you almost nothing but removes the biggest psychological barrier to purchase.
  • Alternatively, frame it as: "Not happy? We'll make it right." — you already offer corrections within 7 days on the How It Works page. Make this a prominent guarantee badge.
  • Put this guarantee directly next to the checkout button, not buried in the FAQ
The difference between "no refunds" and "we'll make it right" is enormous psychologically, even if the practical outcome is nearly identical.
Add-ons at checkout have no clear selection UIhigh impact
The three add-ons (Website Copy, Social Media, Quarterly Refresh) are described well across the site, but I couldn't verify they're selectable at checkout. A proper order summary with tick-box add-on selection at checkout increases average order value significantly.
  • Checkout page should show: core pack (pre-selected, can't deselect) + three optional tick-box add-ons each showing their price
  • Show a running total that updates as they tick/untick
  • Add a one-line benefit reminder next to each add-on checkbox
No exit-intent or scroll-triggered lead capturehigh impact
Most visitors will not buy on the first visit. There is currently no way to capture them before they leave — no email signup, no lead magnet, nothing.
  • Add an exit-intent popup (triggers when the cursor moves toward the browser tab): "Not ready yet? Get the free Sole Trader Legal Checklist" — email capture in exchange for a useful PDF
  • Or a sticky bottom bar: "Free download: The UK Sole Trader Starter Checklist" with an email field
  • Even a simple newsletter signup in the footer ("Monthly tips for UK sole traders") starts building your owned audience
No urgency or scarcity mechanismmedium
The service is evergreen which is fine, but there's nothing nudging a hesitant visitor to act now rather than later. Most people who leave saying "I'll come back" don't.
  • If you have genuine capacity limits (e.g. you can only process X orders per week), show it: "Currently accepting orders — usual delivery within 24 hours"
  • A seasonal angle works too: "Before the self-assessment deadline" is a genuine urgency trigger for sole traders every January
  • Even a simple "Prices may increase as we grow — current price: £79" is honest and effective
Blog CTA says "Get Your Pack for 79" — missing the £ signmedium
On the /blog page, the bottom CTA button reads "Get Your Pack for 79" — the pound sign is missing. Small bug but looks sloppy.
  • Fix to "Get Your Pack — £79" to match every other CTA on the site
Content & Blog
Only 5 blog articles — and they're all from early 2024critical
You have 5 articles, all published in January–February 2024. That's over a year with nothing new. For a site trying to rank for sole trader searches, this signals to Google that the site is inactive.
  • Publish 2–3 new articles per week — this is your single biggest SEO lever
  • Google rewards freshness on evergreen topics like tax, GDPR, and contracts
  • The existing 5 articles are excellent quality — just need volume alongside them
  • Add a "Last Updated" date to each article — keeps them looking current
A site with 5 articles from 18 months ago tells Google: this site has been abandoned. 50 articles, recently updated, tells a completely different story.
Blog has no author bio, no category filtering beyond 2 tags, no estimated read progresshigh impact
The blog articles themselves are thorough but feel structurally bare.
  • Author bio: add a byline at the bottom of each article — photo, name, 2-sentence bio. Builds E-E-A-T for Google.
  • Reading progress bar: a thin bar at the top of the page filling as the user scrolls. Keeps readers engaged.
  • Category filtering: you only have "Legal" and "Financial" — add "Tax", "Invoicing", "Starting Out", "GDPR" as the blog grows
  • Related articles: at the bottom of each post, show 2–3 related articles. Currently there's nothing linking articles to each other.
  • Table of contents: your articles are 20–25 min reads. A sticky or inline TOC at the top would dramatically improve usability.
No newsletter signup anywhere on the sitehigh impact
There is not a single email capture field on the entire website outside of the contact form. This means every visitor who leaves without buying is gone forever.
  • Add an inline signup block mid-article: "Get monthly sole trader guides — no spam, unsubscribe anytime"
  • Add a footer newsletter strip across all pages
  • Use Beehiiv or ConvertKit — both free to start
No "sample document" preview to reduce purchase anxietymedium
Visitors are being asked to pay £79 for documents they've never seen. Showing a redacted sample of one document (e.g. first page of a contract with names/specifics blanked) would dramatically reduce the "but what does it actually look like?" anxiety.

Design & UX
No live chat or chatbot — contact is form-onlyhigh impact
The contact page offers a form, email, and phone number. But someone with a quick pre-purchase question ("does this cover Scottish law?") won't fill in a form and wait 24 hours — they'll just leave.
  • Add Tidio, Crisp, or Tawk.to (all free) as a chat widget — even if it's just you answering manually
  • Or add a simple FAQ chatbot that answers the 10 most common pre-purchase questions instantly
  • A live chat widget on the checkout and pricing pages alone would recover a meaningful number of hesitant buyers
No sticky CTA on mobile — the buy button disappears as you scrollhigh impact
On a long page like What's Included or Pricing, a user who has scrolled down to read the details has to scroll all the way back up to find the buy button. Most won't.
  • Add a sticky bottom bar on mobile that's always visible: "Get Your Pack — £79 →" linking to /checkout
  • Show it after the user has scrolled past the hero (don't show it immediately — feels pushy)
  • This is one of the highest-converting mobile UX changes you can make on a product page

The page has a tab-like navigation (01 · Client Contract, 02 · Terms & Conditions...) but the depth of content under each document is enormous and beautifully written. The problem: visitors don't know how much is there.
  • Add a mini progress indicator: "Document 1 of 10" with a visual progress strip
  • Show a preview snippet under each tab in the nav so users can see there's real content worth reading
  • Consider a "Quick summary" vs "Full breakdown" toggle so time-poor visitors get value without scrolling

Technical & SEO
Canonical URLs still point to foundationary.co.uk — but the site is on .vercel.appcritical
Every page has canonical meta tags pointing to foundationary.co.uk — but the live site is at foundationary.vercel.app. This tells Google the authoritative version is on a domain that doesn't exist, which can suppress your indexing entirely.
  • Either: register foundationary.co.uk and point it at Vercel immediately
  • Or: update all canonical tags to point to foundationary.vercel.app temporarily until the domain is live
  • This is an SEO critical issue — Google may be refusing to index your pages because of this mismatch
The OG image URLs, Twitter card URLs, and sitemap will also all be broken for the same reason. One domain purchase fixes everything.
Contact form has no confirmation state or spam protectionmedium
The contact form appears functional but there's no visible confirmation that submissions go anywhere, and no spam protection (honeypot or reCAPTCHA).
  • Add a success state after form submission: "Thanks — we'll reply within 24 hours"
  • Add a honeypot field (hidden from real users, bots fill it in) to filter spam
  • Consider Formspree or EmailJS if the form isn't already wired to a backend
No cookie consent banner for UK ICO compliancenice to have
If you're using any analytics (Google Analytics, Plausible, etc.) or any third-party scripts, UK PECR regulations require explicit cookie consent. For a site that helps sole traders with GDPR compliance, not having this is particularly notable.
  • Add a minimal cookie consent banner — many free options exist (CookieYes free tier)
  • If you're using Plausible or Fathom (privacy-first analytics), you may be exempt — worth confirming
No analytics visible — no way to know what's workingnice to have
Without analytics you're flying blind on which pages convert, where people drop off, and which blog posts drive the most traffic.
  • Add Plausible Analytics (privacy-first, no cookie banner needed, £9/month) or Microsoft Clarity (free, session recordings)
  • At minimum, connect Google Search Console — it's free and shows you exactly which search queries bring people to your site
